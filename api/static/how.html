How it works

From an idea to its sharpest version

You describe a build. Whetstone runs Path of Building's own damage maths on tens of thousands of versions of it: every skill that fits, every weapon it can use, every ascendancy, every passive worth taking, every support gem, gear crafted to your budget. What comes back is the strongest one it found, in four stages from the campaign to endgame.

The forge, step by step

You describe it

Type what you want to play, like "lightning minion build for a monk, 5 div", or pick it with the buttons. Whetstone reads damage types, kinds of skill, ailments, weapons, classes, ascendancies, skill names and budgets ("5 div", "300 ex", "league starter"), and shows you exactly how it read each word before you forge.

If nothing can match everything you asked for, it doesn't give up. It loosens the request one step at a time (first how strict the theme is, then the ailment, the weapon, the damage type, the kind of skill, the class) and tells you what it changed.

The search

Two rounds, run side by side on several copies of Path of Building:

  • First pass: every skill that fits your idea, on every weapon it can use, with crafted gear at your budget. No passives yet, just raw power, so weak options drop out fast.
  • Second pass: the strongest few skills on each ascendancy, now with full passive trees. When no class is chosen, the best skill is tried on all 23 ascendancies first to find the few that suit it.
  • Deep search (tick it, or ask for something like "highest DPS possible"): the best 24 skills get a second look with passive trees, so a skill that only shines with the right passives isn't cut early. The best 10 are each tried on every ascendancy, and each goes forward on its own best 3. It takes about twice as long and finds builds the normal search can miss.

Builds are scored on maps and bosses together: a build that clears well but can't kill a boss (or the reverse) scores lower than one that does both. A themed build, like bleed or poison, is scored on its theme, and one that gets too little of its damage from it is dropped.

You watch this as the leaderboard on the workbench.

Four anvils

The winner is forged four times at once: Leveling (level 55), Early maps (75), Mid maps (90) and Endgame (95), each capped at your budget. Each stage goes through the same steps:

  • Gear: a rare is crafted for every slot from Path of Building's affix tables: the best base, then damage and defence affixes, then only the resistances still needed. A unique replaces the rare when it scores clearly better and its poe.ninja price fits your budget.
  • Passive tree: the engine scores the whole path to every passive in reach (small ones up to 6 points away, notables and keystones up to 12) and takes the path that adds the most damage and toughness per point, weak passives on the way included. Then it goes back over its own work: it takes back the weakest branches one by one, re-spends those points, and keeps the change only if the whole build gets stronger. That's the "brain" you watch on the workbench, and the order it settles on is the order to take them.
  • Jewels: before the tree, the engine crafts the best jewel this build could socket. Every jewel socket then counts as its path plus that jewel, so the tree travels to sockets exactly when jewels are worth it. Once the build is settled, each socket gets its own jewel, crafted with the others in place, or a unique jewel when one clearly wins and fits your budget. Jewel types and mods come from Path of Building, so they change with the game.
  • Supports: every support gem is screened on your skill, then the best combination is built up one gem at a time, following Path of Building's own rules on what can support what.
  • Spirit and utility: auras, heralds and buffs that fill your spirit, plus curses, marks and banners, are kept only when they raise your damage. A second skill goes on weapon swap when it hits bosses much harder.
  • Every skill kitted out: auras, curses and your movement skill get their own supports too, and each support gem is used once in the whole build, as in game.
  • Mapping and bossing: gear and passives are built for mapping, then a bossing setup (a support swap or a boss skill) is picked against a pinnacle boss.

Stages appear on the page one by one as they finish.

Checks and the rating

The finished build is checked against what you asked for (a bleed build should really bleed), and the endgame stage is rated 1 to 10 on damage, clear and toughness against reference builds for every ascendancy at the same settings. Builds tuned for a theme, speed or loot give up some raw numbers, so they rate a little lower, and that's expected.

Your build

  • The passive tree, drawn on the site, with every point numbered in the order to take it and your ascendancy in the centre.
  • Gear in buy order: line 1 is what to buy first, with affix tiers, unique prices and a "Find on trade" link that opens the official trade site with the stats filled in.
  • Every skill with its supports, the damage mix, the gear cost, and a Path of Building code if you want it in PoB.
  • A share link and a PDF, so you can keep it or send it to a friend.

What the numbers mean

Mapping DPS

Damage per second against normal monsters with your mapping setup. Hits and damage over time both count, and area and projectiles weigh in when ranking builds for clear.

Boss DPS

Single-target damage per second against a pinnacle boss, with the bossing setup (a support swap or a boss skill).

Effective HP

Path of Building's estimate of how much raw damage you can take, counting life, energy shield, armour, evasion and resistances.

Damage mix

How the damage splits by type. Only damage types get bright colours on Whetstone, so you can see what a build scales at a glance.

All numbers are Path of Building's, at the settings the build keeps up (each one is listed with its reason). Use them to compare builds; your in-game numbers depend on your own gear, charges and buffs.

Where it all comes from

Path of Building

The open-source Path of Building Community (MIT licence) does every calculation, run without its window. Its game data covers skills, supports, items, affixes and the passive tree.

poe.ninja

Unique item prices come from poe.ninja, refreshed for the current league. Crafted rares have no fixed price, so each one links to the official trade site instead.

Grinding Gear Games

Item and gem pictures load straight from GGG's own image server. The passive tree is drawn by Whetstone from Path of Building's data, with no game art.

No AI guesswork

Nothing here is a language model's opinion. Reading your prompt is plain keyword matching, and every number is a calculation. Free to use, and it costs nothing to run a build.

What it can't do yet

  • Flasks and charms aren't in builds yet, and Time-Lost (radius) jewels aren't crafted yet. Rare jewels and unique jewels are.
  • The passive tree isn't a perfect search. It reaches notables up to 12 points away and swaps out weak branches afterwards, but a tree has more combinations than any computer could try, so a better tree may still exist.
  • Leveling is one snapshot at level 55, not a step-by-step route through the campaign.
  • Cast on X triggers run on an energy system Path of Building doesn't model. For Cast on Critical, Whetstone uses its own energy maths on Path of Building's numbers: a build gets a Cast on Critical setup when that estimate says it adds damage, and the extra damage is labelled as an estimate, not checked in game. It doesn't yet decide which skill a build uses, and other Cast on X gems and combo finishers aren't modelled yet.
  • Rare item prices are estimates by budget; check the trade links for real prices.
  • Path of Exile 2 1.0 (December 11) needs a Path of Building update first; new classes like the Shadow arrive as soon as it supports them.

Where it runs

This page is always online. The engine itself runs on a home PC, so if it's switched off, the forge says it's cold and new builds wait until it's back. Builds are cached, so asking for one someone already forged comes back instantly.
